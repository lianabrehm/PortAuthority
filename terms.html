1. Scope and acceptance

These Terms and Conditions govern use of the Port Authority demonstration website, proposed customer accounts, product catalog, and logistics coordination features. The platform is intended for small businesses and their authorized representatives. By registering or using account-only functions once enabled, users will be asked to affirmatively accept the current Terms and acknowledge the Privacy Notice. Merely visiting a public informational page does not constitute agreement to an actual transportation contract.

If real transportation services are offered in the future, a separate accepted booking, bill of lading, carrier tariff, or service agreement may govern that shipment. Mandatory law takes precedence where applicable.

2. Port Authority's role

Port Authority is designed as a business-to-business logistics coordination platform. Its proposed services help customers manage shipment information, arrange transportation and delivery through appropriate providers, and organize cargo compliance and security information. Port Authority does not claim to own or operate vessels, trucks, or ports, or to be a licensed customs broker, ocean transportation intermediary, or insurer.

Any future regulated freight forwarding, brokerage, or carriage activity would be undertaken only after required authority is obtained or through appropriately authorized third parties under documented agreements.

3. Customer accounts and authorization

Account registration, login, and profile pages are part of the intended website design; backend authentication is not yet operational. When implemented, each user must provide accurate information, act with authority for the represented business, safeguard credentials, and promptly report suspected unauthorized access. Port Authority may verify business identity and restrict accounts used fraudulently.

Registration should record the accepted Terms version and acceptance timestamp. Whether login will be required to view all pages, rather than only account and ordering features, remains a team decision.

4. Acceptable website use

Users must not attempt unauthorized access, interfere with systems, submit fraudulent orders, impersonate others, upload malicious content, or use the website to arrange illegal transactions. Account and shipment information must be accessed only by authorized users. Automated scraping or testing requires permission unless otherwise allowed by law.

5. Products and proposed services

Goods: Shipping Containers & Cargo Crates; Cargo Security Equipment; and Packaging & Shipping Supplies.

Services: Cargo & Shipment Management; Transportation & Delivery; and Cargo Compliance & Security.

Descriptions, images, prices, and service packages illustrate the proposed offerings. Product availability, warranties, service coverage, and actual delivery arrangements would be specified in accepted commercial terms before live sales.

6. Shopping cart, checkout, and order confirmations

Customers may select goods or services, adjust quantities, review a cart, and proceed through a simulated checkout to an order confirmation page. Customers can complete a pretend payment as part of the checkout demonstration, using fictional payment information only. Displayed totals and payment confirmations are simulated; no real funds are charged, transferred, or collected. Users must not enter real credit card numbers, bank account details, or other genuine payment credentials. A simulated confirmation is not proof that a product has been purchased or a shipment booked.

In a future production service, a request would become binding only when accepted under the applicable booking or sales agreement.

7. Quotations, prices, and charges

Goods and services have displayed prices, which are used to calculate the simulated cart and checkout totals. These prices are illustrative and do not create an obligation to pay real money. Real logistics quotations would depend on shipment dimensions, weight, origin, destination, route, transportation mode, capacity, and requested handling. Applicable charges may include freight, storage, port handling, customs duties, taxes, demurrage, detention, and inspection fees. Any binding quote would identify its validity period, inclusions, exclusions, and payment terms in writing.

8. Customer shipping responsibilities

For a future live service, customers would be responsible for accurate descriptions of cargo, quantity, value, dimensions, gross weight, sender and recipient information, packaging, labeling, and delivery instructions. Customers must disclose special handling needs and provide documents required for lawful transportation. Errors or omissions can cause delays, extra charges, or refusal of a shipment.

9. Customs, import/export, and trade compliance

International shipments may be subject to customs declarations, import/export controls, sanctions, and destination-country requirements. Customers remain responsible for truthful shipment information and required documentation. Port Authority's proposed Cargo Compliance & Security service is informational and coordination-focused; it does not perform licensed customs brokerage. If clearance is required, it would be handled by an appropriately licensed customs broker or other authorized party under separate arrangements.

Port Authority may decline requests involving prohibited transactions or parties subject to applicable restrictions.

10. Dangerous, prohibited, and restricted goods

Customers must disclose hazardous materials, dangerous goods, controlled products, and restricted cargo before any future booking. Acceptance would depend on carrier approval, lawful classification, packaging, documentation, and mode-specific regulations. The demonstration does not accept or transport any physical cargo.

11. Third-party carriers and service providers

Future transportation and delivery coordination may involve independent carriers, freight forwarders, warehouses, and customs brokers. Their separate contracts and legally applicable transportation documents may govern the services they actually perform. Port Authority would communicate relevant provider terms and exercise appropriate care in selecting partners, subject to its contractual obligations.

12. Tracking, transit estimates, and delivery

Shipment management and tracking features are proposed. Any displayed status in the academic prototype is illustrative unless specifically identified as a live integration. Future transit estimates would not be guaranteed delivery dates unless expressly agreed. Weather, port congestion, inspections, and carrier schedules may affect actual movement.

13. Cargo insurance

Cargo insurance is not currently among Port Authority's advertised services and is not sold or arranged through this demonstration. Customers seeking coverage for real shipments would need to obtain it through an appropriate authorized provider. Declaring cargo value alone does not create insurance coverage.

14. Loss, damage, and shipment claims

No physical shipments are performed by the academic platform. For any future live service, customers should promptly report loss, damage, shortage, or delivery discrepancies and preserve photographs, invoices, shipping documents, and delivery receipts. Port Authority's proposed role would be to help route reports to the responsible provider; liability, claim procedures, and deadlines would depend on the applicable contract and mandatory law. No claims deadline or monetary liability cap is established by this demonstration.

15. Returns, refunds, and cancellation

Because checkout payments, orders, and bookings are simulated, a customer may see a pretend charge or payment confirmation, but no real funds are collected and therefore no actual refund is issued or transportation booking canceled. Before live operations, Port Authority would publish product return eligibility, cancellation rules, service fees, and refund procedures and present them before purchase or booking acceptance.

16. Account security, data protection, and privacy

The proposed system will use server-side account controls and a database to manage user and order information. Until these features are implemented and tested, no guarantee of operational authentication, encrypted transmission, or production-grade data security is made. Users should not submit sensitive personal, financial, or real shipping information to the demonstration. For intended information practices and applicable rights, see the Privacy Notice.

17. Chatbot and automated assistance

An AI chatbot is a planned website feature. If enabled, it is intended to provide general navigation and service information, not legally binding shipping quotations, customs advice, or confirmed bookings. Users should verify important information and avoid sharing confidential shipment or personal information in chat unless an appropriate privacy process is implemented.

19. Website accuracy and liability

The prototype is provided for educational demonstration. Descriptions, simulated transactions, and tracking displays should not be relied on for real commercial shipping decisions. Nothing in these terms excludes rights or liabilities that cannot legally be excluded. No arbitrary financial cap, insurance guarantee, or carrier liability allocation is imposed by this draft. A future live contract would require separate legal review.

20. Events beyond reasonable control

For any future transportation contract, events such as natural disasters, government restrictions, port closures, labor disruption, or major infrastructure failures may affect service. Any relief, notice requirements, or allocation of risk would be governed by the applicable service agreement and law.

Once accounts are implemented, Port Authority may restrict access to address fraud, security threats, unlawful activity, or serious violations of these terms, subject to applicable law. Procedures for account closure, retention of necessary records, and review of disputed restrictions should be implemented before launch.

22. Governing law and disputes

Port Authority is a fictional university project and has not selected a legally verified contracting entity, governing-law clause, dispute venue, or transportation contract. These matters must be settled before real commercial use. Future international shipments may also be governed by mandatory transportation statutes, conventions, and carrier terms that cannot be displaced by website terms.

Port Authority may revise these draft terms as the prototype evolves. The version and date should remain visible. Before production use, material changes, acceptance records, and treatment of existing contracts must be handled under applicable law and customer agreements.
